Let's talk about something important – filing your taxes. Now, please note that what I'm about to share isn't financial advice. It's primarily for folks with simple accounting needs, nothing as complicated as bookkeeping, and the like.


Before the age of COVID, my tax routine involved printing out tax forms, which I then physically delivered to my accountant. But before we dive further into this, remember the risks associated with printers. Assume that everything you print could be retrieved from the printer again.


Now, getting back to the main point – COVID changed the game. Tax time was approaching, and my accountant sent me an email stating they'd be accepting virtual filings if needed. Essentially, they were asking me to share all my highly private information via email.


If you're anything like me – someone who values security and privacy and goes to great lengths to safeguard personal information – you might hesitate. It's tough to trust that your accountant is taking the same level of precautions. They're suggesting that you upload files to a random web application or email them directly. Even if you use a link that expires after a while, those files probably end up on their computer alongside data from hundreds of other clients. Sure, we hope none of them are malicious, but can you really take that risk?


Here's a suggestion for accountants: consider hiring someone to guide you on securing your network and files. You might even explore solutions like [Qubes OS](https://www.qubes-os.org){:target="_blank"}. These steps can go a long way in bolstering security.


In the meantime, if your tax situation is relatively straightforward, why not spend a few extra hours and file them yourself? It's certainly easier than dealing with the aftermath of identity theft.


Cheers!
